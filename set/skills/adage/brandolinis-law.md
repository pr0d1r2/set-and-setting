# Brandolini's law

Refuting a false or misleading claim usually costs far more effort than making
it. Protect attention by putting the burden of evidence on the claim and by
choosing corrections for their likely impact.

## Applying Brandolini's law

- Ask for a specific, testable claim and its strongest source before spending
  time on a rebuttal. Do not accept a list of unsupported assertions as one
  unit of work.
- Keep the burden of proof with the claimant. A confident assertion, generated
  text, or plausible explanation is not evidence that others must disprove.
- Triage by reach, harm, audience, and reversibility. Correct a dangerous claim
  influencing a decision before an obscure claim with no practical effect.
- Check the original evidence rather than arguing from summaries. Record the
  source, the exact claim, and the decisive contradiction so others can reuse
  the verification work.
- Correct clearly and proportionately. Lead with the supported account, explain
  the relevant error, and provide a source; avoid repeating a false claim more
  than is needed to identify it.
- Reduce the asymmetry in advance. Require citations, validation, review, and
  smaller claims at publishing boundaries where cheap errors could otherwise
  spread into documentation, code, reports, or decisions.
- Prefer reusable corrections for repeated claims: a test, invariant, minimal
  reproduction, checked example, or short reference can make later review
  cheaper.
- Set a stopping condition. After the consequential claims are checked and the
  evidence is available to the intended audience, do not let an endless stream
  of new assertions consume unlimited time.

## Signals of violation

- A reviewer is expected to disprove every unsupported claim before the author
  supplies evidence for any of them.
- A long list of assertions changes the subject faster than each point can be
  checked.
- Equal effort is spent correcting low-impact trivia and claims that could
  cause costly or irreversible decisions.
- The same misinformation is researched from scratch each time because prior
  evidence and corrections were not preserved.
- A rebuttal amplifies the false claim, buries the supported account, or is much
  harder for the audience to understand than the original assertion.

## Limits

Brandolini's law describes an effort imbalance; it does not show that a claim
is false, excuse dismissing disagreement, or remove the need to correct harmful
misinformation. Do not label an inconvenient or unfamiliar claim as nonsense
to avoid examining its evidence. When health, safety, rights, security, or
other high-impact decisions are at stake, spend the necessary effort and make
the correction accessible. Apply [[truth]] to the evidence and [[consequences]]
to the triage decision.

Based on [Brandolini's law](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brandolini%27s_law),
also known as the bullshit asymmetry principle: refuting misinformation takes
substantially more energy than producing it.
